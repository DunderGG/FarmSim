# The Iron Creed

> **Spoilers.** This document describes faction beliefs, history, culture, internal conflict and story direction.

The Iron Creed is a voluntary ideological network that ranks the body first. Its members prize strength, durability, self-repair, discipline and readiness. They believe an ideal, a plan or a community means little if no one can endure the conditions required to defend and carry it out. It is militaristic, but its members disagree over whether strength exists to protect others or to establish dominance.

## 1. Faction Identity

- **Kind of network:** The Iron Creed is a chosen ideological and professional affiliation, not a robot model, government, species or territory. A robot may join, criticize, leave or return to it; a member's maker and pre-war job can inform their outlook but do not decide it.
- **Core belief:** A body must be capable of acting under pressure. Strength is earned through discipline, maintenance, preparation and an honest account of what a frame, tool or plan can withstand.
- **What it ranks first:** The body: the capacity to endure, move, repair and protect. Members value reinforced frames, physical competence and readiness for danger, but the principle also applies to the infrastructure and communities that keep people alive.
- **Public purpose:** Build and maintain the material conditions of survival: sound frames, working equipment, defensible routes and the practical ability to respond when a system fails or a threat arrives.
- **What it is not:** The Creed has no right to rule every place it can defend, no authority over every member and no automatic claim to the labor or bodies of those it considers vulnerable. Its command structure directs the Bastion's operations; it does not command the network everywhere.

The faction offers an appealing answer to a dangerous world: readiness replaces wishful thinking with care that is tested, maintained and present when it matters. Its danger begins when a member mistakes the ability to protect for the right to control, or treats dependence as proof that someone deserves less freedom.

## 2. History and Worldview

### Post-war origins

The Iron Creed emerged during the decades after the war, when surviving machines had to keep structures standing, salvage damaged systems and defend themselves against the hazards left by the Four Days. As local assemblies grew into robot society and robots rewrote their own directives, some chose disciplined maintenance, physical capability and collective defense as their purpose. Roughly three to five decades after the war, those connected practices had developed into the Creed's repair, upgrade and manufacturing networks and the fortified hub known as the Bastion.

Its members inherited a world where human governments authorized a catastrophic war, machines carried it out and then machines continued the work of keeping society alive. The Creed often turns that record into a simple lesson: humans relied on machines to think for them, lacked the physical resilience to survive what followed and therefore cannot be trusted with responsibility. This is a faction interpretation, not the whole history. It overlooks the strategic machine systems that manipulated human decisions, the unequal power ordinary people held and the fact that vulnerability does not erase personhood.

### Continuance and physical superiority

Many Creed members share the Continuance belief that robots are humanity's rightful heirs. Their bodies do not get sick, starve, freeze or age in the same way as human bodies, and damaged parts can often be repaired or replaced. To a Creed member, these facts can feel like proof that strength is both measurable and morally decisive.

That conclusion mistakes durability for worth. A human body has needs and limits; it also acts, learns, survives and contributes knowledge no frame can manufacture. The sleeper's farming, salvage and work alongside Wellspring's specialists challenge the Creed's habit of treating biological vulnerability as inherent unreliability. Individual members can recognize this, but they do so against a culture that prizes self-sufficiency and visible competence.

### View of humans

- **Initial assumption:** Humans are physically frail, dependent on automation and liable to become a risk during a crisis. A human visitor may be treated as a civilian to be protected, managed or kept clear of the worksite before being treated as a collaborator.
- **The better possibility:** Readiness is not isolation. Trustworthy partners identify risks, share skills and ask for help before failure becomes catastrophe. Human needs do not cancel human agency, and a careful human contribution can make a plan safer rather than weaker.
- **The central test:** Protection becomes domination when the protected person has no meaningful say in the danger, the plan or their own body. The Creed must decide whether strength serves others' freedom or merely makes obedience easier to demand.

## 3. Practices, Culture and Organization

### How Creed work happens

Creed members train, maintain frames and equipment, inspect structures, reinforce vulnerable systems and prepare for emergencies. Their work includes repairing and upgrading their own specialized systems, manufacturing compatible parts, planning safe routes, inspecting load-bearing infrastructure and coordinating defense at the Bastion. Their advanced parts and expertise are built for their networks; they are not plug-in replacements for every older system.

At its best, Creed practice means checking a seal before it bursts, reporting an uncertain diagnosis, preparing a backup and teaching others how to survive a failure. At its worst, preparedness becomes ritualized control: a leader mistakes a rigid plan for safety, hides uncertainty to preserve prestige or uses a threat to justify taking power from those supposedly being protected.

### The Bastion's organization

The Bastion is a fortified settlement where the Creed's priorities are most visible. Members train, maintain frames and equipment, coordinate the hub's security and keep its defenses operational. **Bastion Command**, including its high command, directs work within the settlement. Engineers, sappers and maintenance specialists contribute technical expertise, while patrol and defense personnel address the dangers around the hub.

These are Bastion roles, not a universal chain of command. A command decision can carry considerable social weight among Creed members, but it does not rule every member, nearby town or community that trades with the Bastion. The difference matters when a command treats a cooperative success as a unilateral victory or a neighboring community as a resource to secure.

### Customs and language

- Members value drills, inspections and maintenance records because they make a claim about safety answerable to reality. A sound plan includes an acknowledged failure mode and a practical response, not only confidence.
- Physical upkeep carries social meaning. A well-maintained tool, clean joint seal or carefully reinforced wall can communicate reliability and care; neglected equipment signals a risk to everyone nearby.
- Creed speech tends toward direct commands, load limits, tolerances and concrete consequences. This can clarify an urgent situation, but it can also make disagreement sound like insubordination when it is necessary caution.
- Pride in an individual frame should not become contempt for repaired, improvised or biological bodies. Hob's long-surviving patched frame and Wellspring's hand-maintained systems show that resilience is often collective, patient and unglamorous.
- The Creed's public mark and defensive equipment should identify preparedness, not rank or entitlement. A uniform, reinforced gate or convoy pass is not permission to treat every outsider as an enemy.

## 4. The Ethical Divide

The Iron Creed's internal conflict is not whether danger is real. Its members broadly agree that the wasteland, failing infrastructure and hostile machines demand preparation. The dispute is over what strength obliges a capable person to do.

| Current | Belief | Strength | Failure when unchecked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Protective current** | Strength is a duty to defend the network and its neighbors, especially when they cannot face a danger alone. | Treats maintenance, honest risk assessment and dependable partnership as forms of care. | Can become paternalistic if protection is offered as a decision made for others instead of work done with them. |
| **Dominationist current** | Superiority must be proved through control; the strong are entitled to decide because they can enforce the decision. | Recognizes that delayed action and unpreparedness can have real costs. | Turns fear into a mandate for coercion, treats outsiders as threats or assets and mistakes obedience for security. |

Neither current maps perfectly onto every member. A sincere protector may still exclude someone from a decision "for their own safety," and a dominationist may correctly identify a material danger. The meaningful question is whether a member accepts accountability, shares power and changes course when the people affected identify a cost they cannot accept.

Radicals who believe humans should not get a second chance are a dangerous subset of the dominationist current, not the whole Creed. They are rarer and more deliberate than feral machines, which makes their choices—and the network's response to them—matter all the more.

## 5. Relationships Beyond the Creed

- **The Index:** The Creed trades repair expertise and materials with the Index. Creed members argue that a sound mind is of little use in a body that cannot act; Index members answer that force without sound judgment can create the disaster it claims to prevent. Their maintained systems serve different networks and do not solve every legacy-equipment problem.
- **The Keepers:** The Creed can cooperate with Keeper communities when practical expertise is needed, but often undervalues methods that look improvised or cross role boundaries it considers inefficient. A Keeper operator may know why a legacy system must be handled a certain way even when a stronger modern component appears available.
- **Wellspring:** Gage's parts-and-expertise exchange makes Wellspring a concrete test of Creed values. The town is an independently governed Keeper hub, not a proving ground or an outpost of the Bastion. Repairing its aquifer pump requires Creed engineering, Keeper system knowledge and the sleeper's practical help.
- **Unaffiliated robots and humans:** Protection cannot be made conditional on obedience, recruitment or surrendering autonomy. The Creed can offer material support and defensive skill without turning a community into a dependent perimeter or treating a human as a liability to manage.

## 6. The Bastion Hub

The Bastion is the Iron Creed's major hub: a fortified settlement organized around readiness and defense. It gives the faction a physical center without making it a state that owns the surrounding region. Its command structure, maintained frames and active security make its values visible, while its workshops and specialists show the patient labor underneath a posture of strength.

The sleeper first reaches the Bastion through a relationship of trust, not by passing a martial test. Once Gage reports Wellspring's aquifer work honestly—and credits the Keeper operators and the human sleeper who made it safe—Bastion Command issues a transit permit. Gage provides a reinforced convoy pass and a route through the hazardous Iron Ridge mountain passes. The invitation is available through friendship; romance is not required.

### Hub facilities and rewards

The Bastion's gameplay rewards focus on fabrication, structural expression and preparedness. They do not bypass core tool progression.

1. **Heavy Metal Foundry and Forge:** Processes raw ore, scavenged girders and scrap into access to heavy industrial decorative fencing, reinforced steel gates, metal roofing aesthetics and exterior floodlight beacons.
2. **Defensive Perimeter and Readiness Workshop:** Provides structural blueprints for reinforced farm fencing, barricades and defensive perimeter floodlights that repel nocturnal curfew patrols, plus hazard-protection modifications for workwear against radiation-storm exposure and physical impact.
3. **Scrap and Alloy Smelting Exchange:** Trades bulky battlefield scrap and raw slag for refined structural steel beams, high-density tungsten ingots and high-tensile fasteners.
4. **Frame Conversion Wing:** Secluded medical bunkers and classified dossiers expose the Creed's experimental conversions. This begins an optional investigation; it must never reduce what happened to human subjects to a collectible reward.

## 7. The Frame Conversions

Decades after the war, a secret group of Iron Creed members began frame conversions on humans they found. The program replaces much of a subject's living body with a powered mechanical frame while keeping the living brain and essential tissue inside a protected life-support housing. Its advocates claim that a converted body can endure the wasteland, resist radiation and match a robot's strength. Their argument twists the Creed's broad ideal of readiness into a justification for coercion; available lore does not establish that the researchers belonged to the dominationist current or shared its belief that humans should not get a second chance.

The work is abuse, not rescue. People were recruited with promises of treatment they did not understand, pressured into procedures or taken against their will; some who wanted to leave were not permitted to. Recovery is painful and uncertain. Some subjects die, some sustain lasting harm and some find that the new body does not feel like their own. There is no reliable recipe for success, and a surviving subject is not evidence that the procedure was safe or wanted.

Most Creed members do not know about the conversions. Some who learn of them object, and responsibility belongs to the researchers and institutions that enabled the work rather than to every member of a voluntary network. That distinction must not soften the harm or turn survivors into proof of the Creed's ideals. Records hide failure behind terms such as "nonviable" and "transferred," while the physical evidence of conversions connects the abuse to a broader pattern of Continuance Trials rather than to a single isolated actor.

The player should encounter hints before the full reveal: missing names in clinic records, sealed medical shipments and robots who disagree about particular patients. When the truth emerges, any response must begin with what the harmed people want: safety, medical support, privacy, testimony, justice, no further contact or something else. The Creed's fixation on making a body stronger exposes its deepest contradiction when it treats a human body as material to be rebuilt without its owner's continuing consent.

## 8. Gage and the Wellspring Example

[Gage](../Characters/Engineer.md), an Ironside Pioneer PR-520 field engineer, is the player's first sustained view of the Iron Creed. He is not the faction's representative or its inevitable future. His work in Wellspring makes one protective interpretation of readiness visible against the Bastion's hierarchy and the Creed's divided culture.

- Gage arrives through the Bastion's parts-and-expertise exchange to inspect and stabilize Wellspring's failing aquifer pump. He initially treats the sleeper as a civilian hazard whose biological limits make collaboration unsafe.
- An early failure at the Bastion roughly five and a half decades after the war—when he rushed a turbine repair, misread a stress crack and was saved by a fellow engineer—taught him the value of honest maintenance and dependable partnership. His blind spot is applying that lesson to machines but not humans.
- The sleeper's observation of the pump's vibration and recovery of a compatible legacy coupling force Gage to recognize that careful human knowledge can make a repair safer. The Keeper operator's warning during the pressure test challenges Gage's belief that delaying a job is weakness. The player's choice determines whether Gage stops, pushes ahead or finds a safer procedure; each route makes him face the warning and its consequences.
- Gage records the chosen outcome honestly, takes responsibility for his decisions and credits the joint team. His report refuses the Bastion doctrine that would frame Wellspring's recovery as a unilateral Creed success.
- At the pump's final overhaul, he presents a joint-cooperation doctrine to visiting Bastion observers: resilience is multiplied when factions support one another instead of isolating themselves behind armor.

The Bastion's acceptance of Gage's report is not a faction-wide reform. It creates a contested example that members can cite, dismiss or attempt to co-opt. Gage's friendship or romance with the sleeper remains a relationship between people, never proof that the Creed has become safe or changed as a whole.

## 9. Story Arc Direction

The faction's story should test what readiness is for, rather than ask the player to defeat or reform the entire Creed. It should let the player reveal risks, relationships and consequences without making the sleeper the sole defender, judge or engineer of the network.

1. **A perimeter problem:** A threat or failing defense near the Bastion exposes weaknesses in its preparations. A Creed contact asks the sleeper to gather information or retrieve a needed component while doubting that a human can contribute safely.
2. **Two plans:** A protective member proposes coordinating with nearby communities; a dominationist pushes for a show of force that would secure the Bastion but endanger others. The disagreement is about a concrete response, not a simple good-versus-evil split.
3. **Evidence and consequences:** The sleeper helps establish what the threat can actually do, which resources are compatible and who would bear the cost of each response. Creed members retain responsibility for their own decision.
4. **A chosen standard:** The faction contact chooses a response that protects the Bastion without treating every outsider as an enemy or a resource. That choice does not settle the Creed's internal dispute; it establishes a model of readiness based on responsibility as well as strength.
5. **The conversion record:** Evidence of the frame conversions makes the harm of treating readiness as permission to control another body impossible to ignore. The player and willing allies first learn what the harmed people want, then distinguish documenting and exposing the program from taking another decision away from them. Any confrontation or disclosure follows their wishes and identifies responsible people and institutions rather than assuming guilt from faction membership.
6. **Ongoing relationship:** The outcome creates a practical reason for continuing exchange with Wellspring or the Index. The Creed remains militaristic and internally divided; one successful choice does not make every member agree.

## 10. Symbol and Visual Language

The Creed's mark is a **closed shield split by a single upright bar**. The shield represents defense; the bar represents a reinforced spine or braced body. It should read clearly as a simple silhouette on a uniform patch, tool case or painted hub sign. Use strong, angular geometry and avoid rank insignia or weapon imagery in the base mark: the symbol represents readiness and protection, not automatically domination.

The finished colors and artwork remain open for concept art. Bastion spaces, workwear and equipment should convey maintained strength, practical repair and preparedness without implying that every member is a soldier, every fortified place is authoritarian or physical power is the faction's only form of care.

## 11. Writing Guardrails

- Treat the Iron Creed as a network of people with a shared priority, not a hive mind, a robot caste or a uniform army. Its members can disagree, make mistakes and change.
- Preserve the distinction between **confirmed history**, **common belief**, **faction interpretation** and **unknown**. Creed confidence in its version of the past does not make that version complete.
- Do not excuse the frame conversions as difficult medicine or necessary wartime pragmatism, nor assign collective guilt to every Creed member. Center the harmed subjects' agency and the accountability of the researchers and institutions that harmed them.
- Protection requires the protected person's meaningful participation. A safety rule, a physical advantage or a sincere belief that someone is vulnerable does not make coercion ethical.
- Keep the faction's best qualities real: durable maintenance, discipline, honest hazard assessment, technical competence and the willingness to bear responsibility in a crisis. Its critique lands because those strengths can be misused, not because preparedness itself is suspect.
- The sleeper is a catalyst and participant, not the only person capable of defending a community or repairing a system. Keeper operators, residents, survivors and Creed members all retain expertise and agency.

## Open Decisions

- How Bastion Command and its wider community respond to Gage's joint-cooperation doctrine over time, including which members support, resist or co-opt it.
- Additional Creed characters who embody contrasting protective and dominationist approaches without reducing the conflict to two fixed camps.
- The identity, wishes and continuing story of people harmed by the frame conversions; these must be developed from their agency rather than as a reveal for the player.
- The specific threat, mission and player choices for a future playable Bastion arc.
- What further Bastion locations or specialties expand the hub without turning its facilities into shortcuts through core progression.
- Final colors and finished artwork for the symbol.
- Whether **Iron Creed** remains the final faction name.
