# The engineer

> **Spoilers.** This document describes a character's story and personal arc.

The engineer is an Iron Creed member visiting Wellspring through a parts-and-expertise exchange with the Bastion. Their work on the failing aquifer pump brings them, the Keeper operators and the sleeper into a practical collaboration; engineering is their broader skill, not just their assignment here.

## Character

- **Who:** Name and model undecided. An engineer and voluntary Iron Creed member who came to Wellspring through a parts-and-expertise exchange with the Bastion.
- **Gender and pronouns:** Identifies as a man and uses he/his pronouns. This is his identity, not a property of his heavy-duty frame.
- **Appearance:** A compact, heavy-duty frame built for practical work, with reinforced joints and a few visible field repairs. One forearm carries interchangeable engineering tools and a rugged diagnostic display. Their equipment is orderly and maintained, but not pristine; a restrained Bastion mark identifies their affiliation without making them look like a standard-issue soldier. Their maker and model remain open, and their Iron Creed membership is ideological rather than determined by their chassis.
- **Faction and view of humans:** Values readiness, discipline and protecting others. Believes that humanity's dependence on automated decisions helped bring about the war, and doubts that the sleeper can contribute safely to delicate infrastructure work. This is caution shaped by ideology, not proof that every human is incapable.
- **Backstory:** Volunteered for the exchange to assess the failing aquifer pump and identify what assistance the Bastion could provide. Engineering is not limited to pumps; this is the assignment that brought them to Wellspring. The pump is a Keeper-era legacy system; the engineer can diagnose it, but cannot restore it alone without Keeper operators, compatible parts and coordinated work. They remain in Wellspring to finish the assessment and make a viable plan rather than abandon the commitment.
- **Want:** To return to the Bastion with a precise assessment and a repair plan that can be carried out without risking the town's water supply. They also want to be trusted as a capable engineer, not treated as a symbol of Iron Creed policy.
- **Fear and contradiction:** Fears that a rushed or politically motivated repair could leave Wellspring worse off. They insist that accepting help from the sleeper would be irresponsible, while their own assignment depends on cooperation across roles and communities. They want the pump fixed but initially treat collaboration as a concession rather than a strength.
- **Role in the game:** A technical partner in Wellspring's pump recovery. The sleeper helps gather evidence, locate compatible components and coordinate with Keeper operators; the engineer remains responsible for their own judgments and technical work. The pump is repaired through shared effort, not by the sleeper acting as a lone expert.
- **Personal arc and key events:**
  1. **The assessment:** The engineer inspects the failing pump and tells the sleeper to stay clear; an incorrect intervention could interrupt Wellspring's water supply. The sleeper can accept the boundary or challenge the engineer to explain what evidence would change their mind. The engineer agrees to review useful observations, but remains responsible for the technical work.
  2. **The missing record:** The engineer's initial diagnosis is limited by incomplete maintenance records and incompatible legacy parts. The sleeper helps recover a relevant record or component from the surrounding area. It reveals a risk the engineer had not accounted for, not that humans are inherently better engineers. The engineer revises the plan and acknowledges that dismissing the sleeper also meant dismissing potentially useful evidence.
  3. **The controlled test:** A Keeper operator identifies a safe test that requires coordination between roles. The engineer must choose between delaying for the operator and proceeding with an incomplete test. The sleeper can help bring the parties together and weigh the consequences, but cannot make the technical decision for the engineer. The engineer chooses to wait, and the test exposes a second failure before it can damage the pump.
  4. **The report:** The Bastion expects a concise assessment and may interpret a request for Keeper support as weakness or a failed assignment. The engineer considers understating the uncertainty to protect their standing. The sleeper can encourage candour or focus on the political risk; either way, the engineer decides to report the limitations honestly and propose a joint repair team. The Bastion's response includes an invitation for the sleeper to visit; the engineer shares the route and offers to guide them there, making the hub's location known and opening it as a destination.
  5. **The repair and choice:** Keeper operators, the engineer and the sleeper carry out the agreed repair using compatible parts gathered through the story. The engineer leads the technical plan, while the others contribute their distinct knowledge and work. Afterward, the engineer chooses to recommend continued parts-and-expertise exchanges between Wellspring and the Bastion, treating cooperation as readiness that makes both communities more resilient.
- **Arc outcome:** The pump recovery is a shared achievement, not a test of whether the sleeper can replace trained specialists. The engineer changes their view from excluding a human by default to judging the sleeper's contributions on their merits. The town's trust in the engineer grows because they take responsibility for an honest, cooperative plan.
- **Unlocks and romance:** Friendship reveals the engineer's private notes about the risks they weighed and the pressure they felt to appear certain. Personal events let the player influence how openly the engineer presents the repair plan, but the engineer chooses to own and advocate for it. The engineer is a love interest; romance is an optional path that develops through mutual respect and the engineer's willingness to be vulnerable, not through the sleeper proving their worth by being technically useful.
