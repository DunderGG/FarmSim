# The nomad

> **Spoilers.** This document describes a character's story, personal arc, trade role, dialogue voice and relationship progression.

The nomad is a rare, recurring human visitor who trades unusual wasteland salvage and carefully verified news. They keep a small, fragile circuit of consent-based contacts between scattered survivors, and know the wasteland's routes better than most robots. Their story asks whether independence has to mean carrying every responsibility alone.

## 1. Character Identity

- **Name:** Undecided. Until a final name is chosen, write **the nomad**, not a placeholder name.
- **Gender and pronouns:** Undecided. This sheet uses they/them as a temporary writing convention only; it is not a decision about the character's eventual identity.
- **Age and era:** An adult born after the Four Days. They have no lived memory of the pre-war world and know it only through salvage, advertising and the unreliable stories of robots and survivors.
- **Role:** A route-finder, salvage trader and carrier of carefully verified news. They are not an explorer claiming territory, a representative of all surviving humans, or a guide to a hidden human civilization.
- **Appearance and field gear:** Their travel rig has accumulated over many journeys: dust goggles, a scarf that can cover the nose and mouth, layered weatherproof clothes, sturdy boots, a rolled bedroll and a pack of labelled pouches. Their hand-drawn route book is as important as any piece of salvage, but records hazards, landmarks and agreed signals rather than a complete map to every contact. Their equipment is maintained because a failure on a route can have consequences for more than one person.
- **Mannerisms and voice:** Observant, dryly funny and economical with personal information. The nomad notices water levels, missing trail markers and what has been repaired since their last visit before commenting on the weather. They bargain firmly without exploiting desperate need, and offer practical warnings more readily than comfort. When a question crosses a boundary, they say so plainly rather than inventing a story. Their warmth appears in small acts: returning a repaired tool, remembering a preference or leaving a useful fact with no expectation of payment.

## 2. Worldview and Ideological Conflict

- **Standing:** The nomad belongs to no robot faction and is not a spokesperson for a unified human group. Their circuit includes only a few human refuges and cautious trading stops; it does not prove how many people survive elsewhere.
- **View of humans and the sleeper:** The nomad does not treat the sleeper as a representative of humanity or as an answer to what humans used to be. They are curious about the sleeper's memories, but wary of being treated as an exhibit or as proof that humans can be trusted. They recognize the sleeper's disorientation after waking into a changed world, but will not let that become an excuse to ignore their own boundaries.
- **View of robots:** The nomad does not sort robots into simple allies or enemies. A robot can honour a bargain, and a human can endanger a route. They judge both by what they do with information and trust. They are cautious around systems that record movement or demand identification, especially curfew units and institutions that collect data without explaining its purpose.
- **Formative backstory:** The nomad grew up on one leg of a small human-run circuit between concealed refuges and cautious trading stops. The circuit survived precisely because no one person carried a complete list of people and locations. Older runners taught the nomad to read storm fronts, spot a road that had started drawing curfew patrols, maintain a cache and distinguish useful news from dangerous gossip. When fewer people were able or willing to travel, the nomad took on longer runs. What began as a way to keep their own stop supplied became a livelihood carrying filters, medicine, tools, letters and verified warnings.
- **Core contradiction and flaw:** The nomad fears a failed route, a hostile group or a copied route note exposing someone who expected privacy. They keep contacts compartmentalized and refuse help that comes with demands for names or locations. That caution protects people, but also leaves them carrying problems alone and makes the circuit too dependent on their memory and judgment. They pride themselves on needing no one while depending on fair traders, maintained shelters and people who keep their word.
- **Relationships with other characters:**
  - **The sleeper:** Begins as a customer with an extraordinary past, not a rescue project or a source of pre-war answers. Trust grows when the sleeper honours agreements that give them no immediate reward.
  - **Hob:** The nomad respects Hob's seventy years of practical care but is unsettled by an order that defined a person's entire life. Hob's first-hand knowledge of the farm makes them essential to deciding whether it can become a safe stop; the nomad will not treat that decision as the sleeper's alone.
  - **Gage:** Mutual respect mixed with friction. Gage prefers an inspected, defended corridor; the nomad knows that a visible, well-maintained route can also attract attention. They agree that preparation matters, but disagree about whether security comes from fortification or discretion.
  - **Cleo:** Their conversations expose a useful distinction between recording information and having permission to circulate it. Cleo's arc gives her a basis to understand why the nomad's redactions are not incomplete research; the nomad learns that a carefully limited record can help a community remember dangers without naming the people at risk.
  - **Wellspring:** Most residents know the nomad as a difficult but fair trader. Some are pleased by rare goods and news; others understandably worry that a human circuit could make the town a target. The nomad accepts that concern rather than treating hospitality as owed.

## 3. Route Windows and Availability

The nomad does not live in Wellspring, so a fixed town daily schedule would be false to the character. Their visits should be predictable enough for the player to plan around, but never reduce the route circuit to a permanently open shop or a random event the player can miss.

### Standard Route Window

- **Advance signal:** A mutually agreed marker at the farm gate or trade post shows that the nomad is expected. It appears before their arrival and does not disclose where they came from or where they will go next.
- **Arrival:** On clear or manageable-weather days, the nomad reaches the farm in late afternoon, establishes a small trade camp near the agreed stop, and remains through the following day. The player can trade, talk or accept available requests during that window.
- **Departure:** If the player does not engage, the nomad leaves a short, non-sensitive note stating when to expect the next route window. They never punish the player for missing a visit, and essential progression never depends on catching one.
- **Cadence:** Early visits are authored story meetings. After the first meeting, regular route windows should be driven by a visible seasonal or story cadence, not pure random chance. Exact timing and stock rotation belong to the future character, dialogue and trading-system design.

### Radiation Storms, Heavy Dust and Winter

- **Hazardous weather:** The nomad does not arrive during a radiation storm or heavy dust event. A pending visit is postponed, not cancelled, and the marker remains as a clear promise that the player has not missed content.
- **If already present:** The nomad uses the agreed shelter or Wellspring's public space, trades only when it is safe, and offers route observations after the storm. They do not ask the player to travel into unsafe weather merely to preserve a delivery schedule.
- **Winter:** Winter runs are less frequent and more deliberate. The nomad carries more repair material, preserved food and weatherproof gear, while their dialogue emphasizes closed passes, ice, short daylight and the value of a maintained shelter.

## 4. Trade, Item Preferences and Gifting

The nomad's trade is a supplement to farming, Wellspring's shops and exploration. It offers unusual, flavourful or convenience-focused goods without becoming the required source of seeds, core machines, tool tiers or story-critical materials.

### Trade Stock

- **Field supplies:** Weatherproof clothing pieces, repair kits, spare filter cartridges, compact lanterns, durable rope and other travel equipment.
- **Unusual salvage:** Small batches of pre-war domestic hardware, unusual decorative components, intact dials, brass fittings and curios with clear provenance.
- **Optional leads:** Verified reports of changed hazards, abandoned caches and salvage opportunities in already available or optional wasteland areas. A lead is never a direct route to a concealed human refuge.
- **Player trade-ins:** Clean farm produce, preserved trail food, repaired tools, accurately labelled salvage and materials that can safely be passed onward. The nomad pays fairly for useful goods rather than requiring gifts disguised as trade.
- **Stock boundaries:** Their inventory is limited, opportunistic and data-driven. Missing an item changes a convenience or optional opportunity, never the player's ability to progress through farming, town restoration or the core tech tree.

### Item Preferences and Gifting

Gifts build friendship but do not purchase access to private information. The nomad values things that make a journey safer, reduce waste or show that the player understands the difference between useful knowledge and someone else's property.

- **Loved gifts (+80 Friendship):**
  - *Sealed Water Filter Cartridge:* A tested replacement cartridge with its capacity and origin marked clearly.
  - *Reliable Hand Compass:* A simple, repairable compass that does not depend on a network or a charged screen.
  - *Stormproof Field Lantern:* A protected, low-draw lamp useful in a shelter or on a night crossing.
  - *Hand-Bound Field Book:* Durable blank pages with a weatherproof cover, suitable for recording observations without exposing an existing route book.
- **Liked gifts (+45 Friendship):**
  - *Preserved Trail Meal:* Clearly dated, safely stored farm food that can travel well.
  - *Mending Kit:* Needles, strong thread, patches and wax for repairing clothing and packs.
  - *Accurately Labelled Salvage:* A working component accompanied by an honest note about where it was found and what it does.
  - *Dried Medicinal Herb:* Farm-grown, properly identified herbs for a first-aid kit or trade.
  - *Clean Canvas or Oilcloth:* Durable material for wrapping gear, making a shelter patch or protecting a cache.
- **Neutral gifts (+20 Friendship):**
  - Common clean crops, stone, clay, ordinary timber and basic metal stock.
- **Disliked gifts (-20 Friendship):**
  - *Unlabelled Medicine:* Pills, syringes or compounds with no reliable dose or source.
  - *Decorative Fragileware:* Heavy glass ornaments and delicate display pieces that will not survive a pack.
  - *Unsorted Scrap:* A bag of mixed, dirty fragments offered without knowing whether any of it works.
- **Hated gifts (-50 Friendship):**
  - *Stolen Personal Letter or Photograph:* A private item taken from a marked home or shelter and treated as casual currency.
  - *Copied Route Notes:* Directions, contact names or identifying marks taken without the owner's permission.
  - *Unsealed Cinderium Residue:* Hazardous material that risks contaminating a person, shelter or supply cache.

### Gift Dialogue Responses

- **Loved:** *"You labelled the capacity, the source and the date. That means I can hand it to someone else without asking them to trust a guess. That's worth more than the cartridge."*
- **Liked:** *"Good choice. It is useful, it travels, and I know what it is. You'd be surprised how rare all three are."*
- **Neutral:** *"Nothing wrong with it. Nothing a route depends on, either. Thank you."*
- **Disliked:** *"If I cannot tell what it is, I cannot safely trade it on. Keep it until someone can give it a name."*
- **Hated:** *"No. Someone trusted a place, a person or a page with this. That does not make it yours to hand over."*

## 5. Relationship Progression and Narrative Arc

Friendship progresses through 10 trust tiers. Every change should be visible in the nomad's language, the information they volunteer and the care they take with the farm as a stop. Gifts and fair trades can support progress, but story trust comes from respecting boundaries, keeping an agreement and helping without demanding ownership of the circuit.

```
[0-2 Hearts: The Customer] --> [3-4 Hearts: The Known Stop] --> [5-6 Hearts: The Trusted Relay]
                                                                              |
                             [9-10 Hearts: The Open Route] <-- [7-8 Hearts: The New Stop]
```

### Stage 1: Hearts 0-2 - The Customer

- **Attitude:** Alert, professional and courteous without being familiar. The nomad will trade fairly, but gives the sleeper no more personal information than any other new customer.
- **Dialogue:** Refers to the player as "sleeper" only because that is how Wellspring knows them, then asks what name they prefer. They answer personal questions briefly and redirect toward the immediate trade.
- **Key flavor line:** *"A safe route is not a line on a map. It is the next dry roof, the next working pump and the next person who keeps a promise. Maps do not do any of that."*

### Stage 2: Hearts 3-4 - The Known Stop

- **Attitude:** Cautiously more open. The nomad recognizes that the farm's improvements are real and begins to see the sleeper as someone who can be relied upon in a limited, practical way.
- **Dialogue:** Shares observations about changed roads, storm behaviour and trade goods. They start to leave useful warnings without being asked, but still protect names, locations and messages.
- **Key Event 1 - The First Measure:**
  - **Trigger:** Reach 3 Hearts and speak to the nomad during a standard route window.
  - **Scene:** The nomad asks the sleeper to hold a sealed weather tube in the agreed trade shelter until the next route window. They make clear that the player may refuse; the test is whether the sleeper can honour a small boundary, not whether they will obey an order.
  - **Player role:** Accept the limited responsibility, decline it honestly or ask what safeguards make the request fair. The player is never entitled to open the tube or learn its recipient.
  - **Outcome:** The nomad explains why the tube has no name or destination on it. If the player accepted, it is collected sealed; if not, the nomad accepts the answer and seeks another arrangement. Either path can establish trust through honesty, but opening it is a clear breach.
- **Key Event 2 - The Silent Marker:**
  - **Trigger:** Reach 4 Hearts; a familiar route signal does not receive its expected reply.
  - **Scene:** The nomad cannot tell whether a contact moved, a storm destroyed a cache or someone has begun watching the route. They ask the sleeper to help investigate the surrounding wasteland for physical evidence, not to pursue a missing person.
  - **Player role:** Recover a broken marker, compare its damage with recent weather and identify whether it was exposed naturally or tampered with. The player learns to distinguish evidence from speculation.
  - **Outcome:** The evidence suggests that an old route note may have been copied. The nomad thanks the sleeper for helping establish what is known rather than pressuring them to invent certainty.

### Stage 3: Hearts 5-6 - The Trusted Relay

- **Attitude:** Franker and occasionally vulnerable. The nomad admits that their caution has become a burden, but does not mistake friendship for a right to another person's history.
- **Dialogue:** Uses the sleeper's chosen name consistently. They ask for the player's assessment of a risk and listen to the answer, even when they disagree.
- **Key Event 3 - The Copied Page:**
  - **Trigger:** Reach 6 Hearts and return to the damaged marker site with the nomad.
  - **Scene:** A piece of a route note has reached someone seeking access to the circuit's information without accepting responsibility for the people named by it. The immediate danger is exposure, not a fight to be won.
  - **Conflict:** Closing the route protects the contacts but cuts off supplies and warnings. Broadcasting every detail may alert the wrong people. Keeping the problem to themself repeats the nomad's old habit of carrying it alone.
  - **Player role:** The sleeper can offer farm records, a safe place to prepare replacement signals or a perspective on what consent means after a secret has already been compromised. They cannot demand the contacts' names or decide who receives a warning.
  - **Outcome:** The nomad chooses to warn the people who need to know, replace the compromised code and ask a few trusted stops to share the work. It is an imperfect solution with real cost, but it is the first time they deliberately distribute responsibility.

### Stage 4: Hearts 7-8 - The New Stop

- **Attitude:** Deep friendship and practical trust. The nomad is still private, but no longer treats distance as the only form of safety.
- **Key Event 4 - The Farm Agreement:**
  - **Trigger:** Reach 8 Hearts after the copied-page event; speak with the nomad at the farm gate near sunset.
  - **Scene:** The nomad asks whether the farm can become a limited, explicitly agreed stop for rest, trade and warnings. Hob, the sleeper and Wellspring each have a legitimate reason to be cautious. The nomad refuses to promise access to concealed communities in return.
  - **Player role:** Help make the terms concrete: identify a safe public place, establish what supplies may be held, and make clear that anyone using the stop can decline to share information. The player cannot grant consent for Hob, Wellspring or absent contacts.
  - **Outcome:** After every affected party is heard, the nomad chooses to add the farm to the circuit under limited rules. The farm becomes a place people can use by agreement, not a headquarters, permanent camp or shortcut to a hidden settlement.
- **Unlock:** More dependable visits, a wider selection of optional field stock and the **Route Ledger** feature described below. This does not unlock a human faction hub or grant coordinates to private homes.

### Stage 5: Hearts 9-10 - The Open Route

- **Attitude:** Relaxed, affectionate in a platonic way and willing to let the player see the labor behind a route. They remain a traveller by choice and do not become a resident, farmhand or spouse.
- **Key Event 5 - The Shared Burden:**
  - **Trigger:** Reach 10 Hearts; complete a later route window after the farm agreement.
  - **Scene:** The new circuit is tested by an ordinary but stressful disruption: a storm closes a crossing, a cache needs restocking and two stops receive conflicting reports. The nomad brings the information to the people who can act on it instead of silently taking every task themselves.
  - **Player role:** Help maintain the farm's agreed supplies and offer observations from the local area. The player can see that their farm is useful precisely because its owners decide what help they can safely give.
  - **Outcome:** The circuit no longer rests on one traveller's memory. Trusted stops can confirm hazards, hold supplies and pass only the information their owners have agreed to share. The nomad leaves on the next run with a copied, deliberately incomplete route book and the knowledge that asking for support is not surrendering freedom.
- **Romance:** The nomad is **not** a romance option. Their complete arc is a lasting friendship built on boundaries, reciprocal support and the freedom to keep travelling. It does not lead to dating, marriage or moving to the farm.

## 6. Friendship Support and Farm Presence

The nomad never moves into the farmhouse, becomes a permanent shopkeeper or takes a farm labour role. Their support must feel like an extension of the relationship and their route work, not a replacement for Wellspring's services or the player's own progress.

- **Route trade chest:** After the farm agreement, the player can leave clearly labelled, non-essential trade goods in an agreed chest. The nomad collects or reciprocates only during a later route window; it is not an instant delivery system.
- **Route reports:** The nomad may annotate the Route Ledger with recently verified hazards, a changed safe crossing or a useful salvage opportunity. They do not replace the weather broadcaster's forecast or reveal private destinations.
- **Safe-stop upkeep:** Occasionally asks for small, optional supplies such as clean water, preserved food, oilcloth or replacement lantern parts. Completing these requests improves the stop's appearance and trade variety but never gates essential content.
- **Practical gifts:** At high friendship, the nomad can leave useful but non-essential field items, repaired versions of things the player traded, or small pieces of route wisdom. Their gifts should favor preparedness and exploration over raw power.

## 7. Route and Trade Unlocks

The nomad opens no human hub. Their rewards make the world feel more connected without contradicting the rarity and privacy of surviving humans, and they stay within the relationship track rather than gating core machines or town restoration.

1. **The Route Ledger:** A farm-side journal or board showing only player-safe information: confirmed hazards, open crossings, changes after storms and optional cache leads. It never marks a human refuge, a private contact or the nomad's next destination.
2. **Field Exchange Stock:** At higher friendship, the nomad brings a larger rotation of optional travel supplies, decoration pieces and rare salvage. It remains limited, seasonal and non-essential.
3. **Safe-Stop Details:** The farm gains modest visual additions such as an agreed lantern hook, water barrel, sheltered bench or anonymous trade marker. These show that the stop exists without turning the farm into a public market.
4. **Wasteland Requests:** Optional errands recover marked salvage, restock a public cache or verify a route condition. Their rewards are trade value, lore, cosmetic items or new optional leads, never access to a private settlement.
5. **Trustworthy News:** Late friendship unlocks richer stories about life on the road and the nomad's care in verifying information. It does not answer the world's unknown human population or resolve the sleeper's family mystery by coincidence.

## 8. Technical and Implementation Notes

- **Content data:** When the planned character-content system is designed, the nomad should be represented by a character asset using the project's namespaced ID convention (for example, `Base.Nomad`). Their trade inventory, gifts, dialogue and visit conditions must be data-driven; no list of stock items, contacts or routes belongs in character code.
- **System guide first:** The character, dialogue, gifting, trader and schedule systems are not built yet. Before implementation, add the required system guide to `Docs/Systems.md` and decide the owner of each responsibility. Do not add an all-purpose component to the nomad actor simply because it is convenient.
- **Visit logic:** Store the current story stage, friendship, visit availability and stock rotation in the appropriate save-owned character or world state. Visits must be authored or visibly scheduled, never generated by an opaque random check that can make the player wait indefinitely.
- **Privacy boundary:** The route ledger may store anonymized, player-safe route conditions only. Hidden-contact names, precise locations and the complete circuit must not enter general map, quest or UI data where unrelated systems can reveal them accidentally.
- **Content loading:** Trade goods and dialogue assets use content IDs and soft references, loaded ahead of a scheduled visit rather than synchronously when the player opens the trade menu.
- **Performance:** Route-window updates occur on story, day or weather events. The nomad actor, HUD and trade UI do not search the world, rebuild stock or poll route state every frame.

## 9. Key Event Dialogue Scripts

These scripts establish dialogue voice and the player-facing limits of the nomad's relationship. They are writing references, not final localization text.

### Event 1: The First Measure (3 Hearts)

**Location:** Farm gate trade shelter. Late afternoon (17:20). Clear weather.
**Condition:** The player approaches the nomad after reaching 3 Hearts during a route window.

> **NOMAD:**
> *(They set a thumb-length metal weather tube on the counter, then keep two fingers resting on it.)*
> "I have a small ask. This stays under that dry eave until I return. No name on it, no destination. If that makes it a bad ask, say so now. I will find another way."
>
> **PLAYER DIALOGUE CHOICES:**
>
> * **[Accept the boundary]:** *"I can keep it dry and leave it sealed."*
>   * **NOMAD:** *(Their hand leaves the tube.)* "That is all I need. Not blind trust. A clear agreement."
>
> * **[Ask about the risk]:** *"What happens if someone finds it?"*
>   * **NOMAD:** "Then it is a weather tube someone found. Nothing in it names a person or a place. I do not ask you to take a risk I have not planned for."
>
> * **[Decline honestly]:** *"I am not comfortable holding something I cannot inspect."*
>   * **NOMAD:** *(They slide the tube back into an inner pocket without offense.)* "Fair answer. Better than saying yes and resenting it halfway through."

### Event 3: The Copied Page (6 Hearts)

**Location:** Damaged roadside relay shelter. Late morning (10:40). The storm has passed, but copper dust lies in the cracks.
**Condition:** The player and nomad have recovered the silent marker and return to inspect the site.

> **NOMAD:**
> *(They hold the torn corner of a page beside the route book without letting the two touch.)*
> "This is enough. A shape, a mark, the sort of turn someone remembers once they have seen it. Not enough to find anyone today. Enough to make every next run less safe."
>
> **PLAYER DIALOGUE CHOICES:**
>
> * **[Offer a warning]:** *"We can help people change their markers before anyone follows the old ones."*
>   * **NOMAD:** "We can. I keep thinking I should do every leg myself. That is how a route turns into a single point of failure."
>
> * **[Suggest closing the route]:** *"Then close it. Safety first."*
>   * **NOMAD:** "Sometimes that is the right call. This time, closing it without a warning leaves people waiting for water filters that will never come. I need a better answer than disappearing."
>
> * **[Ask for the names]:** *"Tell me who is at risk. I could help more."*
>   * **NOMAD:** *(Their expression firms, not angry but final.)* "You can help without a name. If you need one before the help counts, you are asking for something I cannot give."

### Event 4: The Farm Agreement (8 Hearts)

**Location:** Farm gate. Sunset (20:10). The first safe-stop lantern is unlit beside the shelter.
**Condition:** The copied-page event is complete and the player speaks with the nomad at the beginning of a route window.

> **NOMAD:**
> "A stop is not a favor someone grants once. It is rules, supplies, people who can say no and still be welcome the next time. I think this farm could be one. I also think that is not mine to decide alone."
>
> **HOB:**
> *(They inspect the unlit lantern, then look to the sleeper.)*
> "The farm has been preserved by one instruction for a very long time. I would prefer its next purpose to be chosen with care."
>
> **PLAYER DIALOGUE CHOICES:**
>
> * **[Support a limited stop]:** *"We can offer rest and trade. Nobody has to give more than that."*
>   * **NOMAD:** "That is the right shape of it. Useful, not possessive."
>
> * **[Ask for clear terms]:** *"Then let us write the terms before anyone uses it."*
>   * **NOMAD:** *(A small, relieved smile.)* "A route runner after my own heart. Clear terms save more trouble than clever promises."
>
> * **[Set a boundary]:** *"The farm cannot take every risk the route carries."*
>   * **NOMAD:** "It should not. A good stop knows its limits. We will build around the limits you can live with, or we will not build it."

## 10. Seasonal and Festival Dialogue

The nomad's dialogue reflects a person who reads the world as a changing route, appreciates ordinary safety and does not romanticize hardship.

### Daily Seasonal Dialogue

- **Spring:** *"The ground looks easier after rain. It is not. Wet soil hides soft edges, and soft edges take boots, wheels and sometimes people. Test a path before you trust it."*
- **Summer:** *"Carry water before you feel thirsty. By then you are already behind, and the heat has had all morning to make a liar of your plans."*
- **Autumn:** *"A full pantry is not just comfort. It lets people say no to bad bargains in winter. That is a kind of freedom."*
- **Winter:** *"The short route is not always the fast one. In winter, the fast route is the one with a door that still closes and someone who knows you are coming."*

### Festival Responses

- **Spring - Hearthwell Day:**
  *"A product launch became a holiday. I have seen stranger things survive than the people who started them. At least the lanterns are pretty."*
- **Summer - Founders' Fair:**
  *"Empty stalls, a brass band and three speeches about civic harmony. I expected nothing useful and found a spare gasket at the tool swap. A successful fair, then."*
- **Autumn - Harvest Home:**
  *"This is the one I understand. Food put away, tools shared, everyone pretending they did not worry all season. Enjoy it. You earned the easy evening."*
- **Winter - The Great Clearance:**
  *"A sale is only a bargain if you need the thing, can carry it and it will still work next month. I brought you a warm scarf. It meets all three tests."*
