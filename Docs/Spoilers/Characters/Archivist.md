# The Index archivist

> **Spoilers.** This document describes a character's story, personal arc and role in opening a faction hub.

The archivist is an Index researcher who visits Wellspring to seek records of its pre-war water system. Their story asks who owns the record of a community's past; the archivist's answer leads them to invite the sleeper to the Archive and provide a safe route there.

## Character

- **Who:** Name and model undecided. An Index archivist or field researcher visiting Wellspring from the Archive.
- **Gender and pronouns:** Identifies as a woman and uses she/her pronouns. This is her identity, not a property of her light frame.
- **Appearance:** A light, adaptable frame suited to carrying records and working in the field, with a compact scanner and a weatherproof folio or storage case for copied documents. Several small lenses or a movable optical assembly give them a habit of looking closely at objects and people; their careful, annotated equipment contrasts with the simplicity of Wellspring's legacy systems. Any Index insignia is subtle. Their maker and model remain open, and Index membership is ideological rather than determined by their chassis.
- **Faction and view of humans:** Values memory, evidence and careful interpretation. The archivist initially sees the sleeper as a valuable witness to pre-war life, but risks treating a person as a source before considering their consent or perspective.
- **Backstory:** Sent to document Wellspring's aging water system and its pre-war aquifer station. The Index's advanced systems cannot simply replace the town's Keeper legacy equipment, so the archivist needs local operators and surviving records to understand how it was meant to work.
- **Want:** To return to the Archive with accurate records that preserve both the system's technical history and the context in which Wellspring uses it.
- **Fear and contradiction:** Fears that incomplete or poorly contextualized records will mislead future researchers. They value evidence, yet initially assume that collecting more information is automatically worthwhile, even when the people involved have not agreed to be studied.
- **Role in the game:** A contact between Wellspring and the Index. The archivist requests help finding and interpreting fragmented water-system records. The sleeper works with local residents to recover and contextualize them; the archivist contributes research methods and does not replace Keeper operators or repair the pump.
- **Hub discovery:** When the sleeper helps recover the records and establish what Wellspring is willing to share, the archivist chooses to offer an invitation to the Archive and provide a safe route there. This reveals the Index hub as a visitable destination; it is not information the archivist gives away before trust or an exchange has been established.
- **Personal arc and key events:**
  1. **The request:** The archivist asks the sleeper for memories of the pre-war world and access to Wellspring's pump records. They assume that preservation is an unquestionable good. The sleeper can answer cautiously or ask how the information will be used; the archivist explains their purpose but has not yet considered who should decide what is shared.
  2. **The incomplete archive:** The recovered records conflict with each other and omit local knowledge held by Keeper operators. The sleeper helps connect the archivist with residents who can interpret them. The archivist learns that records are not neutral when context and the people who maintain the system are missing.
  3. **The privacy dispute:** Some residents agree to share technical records but object to personal testimony or details that could expose Wellspring to outside pressure. The archivist initially argues that withholding evidence risks future misunderstanding. The sleeper can help both sides clarify concerns, but the archivist must decide whether to respect the limits set by the people sharing their history.
  4. **The invitation:** The archivist chooses to return only the agreed material, with its uncertainties and local context intact, rather than take everything they can collect. They invite the sleeper to the Archive to see how the records will be preserved and interpreted, and provide a safe route there. This is the character-driven reveal that makes the Index hub a visitable destination; it is not dependent on starting a romance.
  5. **The Archive's record:** At the Archive, the archivist presents the Wellspring records with attribution, context and the community's restrictions. They accept scrutiny from their peers and argue that ethical limits make the archive more trustworthy, not less complete. They choose to establish an ongoing, consent-based exchange with Wellspring.
- **Arc outcome:** The archivist shifts from treating knowledge as something to collect toward accepting responsibility to the people whose lives it describes. The sleeper helps surface perspectives and negotiate understanding, but the archivist makes and defends the decision. Wellspring gains a relationship with the Index without being reduced to research material.
- **Friendship and romance:** Friendship reveals the archivist's private doubts about whether their work has served the people whose histories they preserve. The archivist is a love interest; romance is optional and grows from mutual curiosity, trust and respect for boundaries. It does not require the sleeper to disclose personal memories or to agree with the archivist's research.
