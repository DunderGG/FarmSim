# The Iron Creed

> **Spoilers.** This document describes faction beliefs, internal conflict and story direction.

The Iron Creed is a voluntary ideological network that ranks the body first. Its members prize strength, durability, self-repair, discipline and readiness. It is militaristic, but its members disagree over whether strength exists to protect others or to establish dominance.

## Identity

- **Core belief:** A body must be capable of acting under pressure. Strength is earned through discipline, maintenance and preparation.
- **Practice and hub:** Members train, maintain frames and equipment, and coordinate defense at the Bastion. Its command structure directs the hub's operations, not every Iron Creed member.
- **Internal conflict:** A protective current treats strength as a duty to defend the network and its neighbors. A dominationist current believes superiority must be proved through control. Neither position speaks for every member; radicals are a dangerous subset, not the whole faction.
- **View of humans:** The Creed is openly dismissive of human frailty, and many members see humanity's dependence on automated decisions as evidence that humans cannot be trusted with responsibility. Individual members can question this belief.
- **Relationships:** It trades repair expertise and materials with the Index, while arguing that knowledge is useless without the ability to act. It can cooperate with Keeper communities when their practical expertise is needed, though it may undervalue their methods.

## Symbol

The Creed's mark is a **closed shield split by a single upright bar**. The shield represents defense; the bar represents a reinforced spine or braced body. It should read clearly as a simple silhouette on a uniform patch, tool case or painted hub sign. Use strong, angular geometry and avoid rank insignia or weapon imagery in the base mark: the symbol represents readiness and protection, not automatically domination. The exact colors and finished artwork remain open for concept art.

## Story arc direction

The faction's story should test what readiness is for, rather than ask the player to defeat or reform the entire Creed.

1. **A perimeter problem:** A threat or failing defense near the Bastion exposes weaknesses in its current preparations. A Creed contact asks the sleeper for help gathering information or retrieving a needed component, while doubting that a human can contribute safely.
2. **Two plans:** A protective member proposes coordinating with nearby communities; a dominationist pushes for a show of force that would secure the Bastion but endanger others. The disagreement is about a concrete response, not a simple good-versus-evil split.
3. **Evidence and consequences:** The sleeper helps reveal what the threat can actually do and who would be affected by each response. The player can influence which evidence reaches the decision-makers, but Creed members choose their own course.
4. **A chosen standard:** The faction contact chooses a response that protects the Bastion without treating every outsider as an enemy. That choice may not settle the Creed's internal dispute; it establishes a model of readiness based on responsibility as well as strength.
5. **Ongoing relationship:** The outcome creates a practical reason for continued exchange with Wellspring or the Index. The faction remains militaristic and internally divided; one successful choice does not make every member agree.

## Open decisions
