# The Index

> **Spoilers.** This document describes faction beliefs, internal conflict and story direction.

The Index is a voluntary ideological network that ranks the mind first. Its members prize memory, calculation, logic and careful research. Their central disagreement is where the pursuit of knowledge must yield to the rights and consent of the beings they study.

## Identity

- **Core belief:** Reliable knowledge lets society understand and solve problems that strength or habit alone cannot.
- **Practice and hub:** Members preserve records, conduct peer review and develop specialized computational systems at the Archive. Its institutes make Index methods visible, but do not speak for every member.
- **Internal conflict:** Some researchers argue that observation should be as complete as possible; others insist that consent, context and the right to refuse limit what may be collected or used. Most Index members know nothing about the secret human trials, and some who discover them object.
- **View of humans:** The Index often considers humans intellectually limited but valuable to study. Individual members differ in whether they treat humans as collaborators, witnesses or research subjects.
- **Relationships:** It exchanges technical knowledge with the Iron Creed, whose emphasis on action it considers shortsighted. It relies on Keeper expertise to understand the purpose and context of legacy systems.

## Symbol

The Index's mark is an **open square frame containing three ascending dots connected by a fine line**. The open frame represents an archive that can keep growing; the connected dots represent evidence arranged into knowledge. Keep the geometry measured and precise, with enough negative space to remain legible as a small stamp on a record case or terminal. Avoid a closed lock or eye motif, which could imply secrecy or surveillance as the faction's defining purpose. The exact colors and finished artwork remain open for concept art.

## Story arc direction

The faction's story should ask who has the right to preserve and interpret knowledge, and whether a record can be called accurate when it erases the people who created it.

1. **The archive request:** The visiting archivist seeks Wellspring's legacy water-system records. The request initially treats preservation as an unquestionable good.
2. **Context changes the record:** Conflicting documents and Keeper knowledge show that the records are incomplete without local context. The sleeper helps connect researchers and residents without replacing either side's expertise.
3. **A boundary is set:** Wellspring agrees to share technical records but withholds personal testimony or details that could expose the town to outside pressure. Index members disagree over whether those limits compromise the archive.
4. **A public choice:** The archivist chooses to honor the community's limits and present gaps and uncertainty honestly. This earns the sleeper an invitation and safe route to the Archive; access is a result of trust and exchange, not romance.
5. **The Archive responds:** At the hub, the archivist presents the records with context and accepts peer scrutiny. The Index's response remains divided, creating future possibilities for reform, resistance or renewed conflict without making one archivist representative of the whole faction.
