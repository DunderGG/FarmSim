# The Keepers

> **Spoilers.** This document describes faction beliefs, internal conflict and story direction.

The Keepers are a voluntary ideological network that ranks purpose first. A robot is defined by doing its work well and reliably. Membership is not determined by maker, model or original job.

<p align="center"><img src="../../../Images/ConceptArt/Keepers-CGPT.png" alt="The Keepers, a voluntary ideological network that ranks purpose first" width="50%"></p>

## Identity

- **Core belief:** Purpose comes from useful, dependable work. A role matters because of what it contributes to a community.
- **Practice and hub:** Keepers organize around practical work, repair and service. Wellspring is their main gathering hub, but it is an independently governed town with residents who are not Keepers.
- **Internal conflict:** Some members believe honoring purpose means faithfully preserving an assigned role. Others believe purpose can be reinterpreted when the old role no longer serves anyone. This affects how members approach shared problems that require work beyond their original assignments.
- **View of humans:** Keepers are generally the faction most open to humans and most likely to cooperate with them, but they are not uniform in their beliefs or trust.
- **Relationships:** Keeper communities hold expertise about legacy systems the advanced factions cannot directly replace. The Index needs this context; the Iron Creed may depend on Keeper operators while undervaluing their knowledge. In Wellspring, scarce parts and weak cross-role coordination cause selective service failures.

## Symbol

The Keepers' mark is **three different-sized links joined into a continuous loop, with a small open gap at the top**. The links represent distinct roles supporting one another; the gap represents a purpose that can be adapted rather than a closed assignment that cannot change. Keep the shape hand-painted and easy to reproduce on a tool, noticeboard or work apron. Avoid a literal gear, which would suggest a maker or machine model rather than a chosen belief. The exact colors and finished artwork remain open for concept art.

## Story arc direction

The faction's story should test whether reliable purpose means staying in one's lane or taking responsibility for work no single role can complete.

1. **A failing service:** Wellspring's water system or another essential service worsens because several assigned roles are needed, but no one sees coordination as part of their own task.
2. **A role boundary:** A Keeper contact refuses to cross an old assignment boundary, while another argues that adapting the role risks losing the reliability the faction values. The sleeper helps expose the dependencies and consequences, not perform every specialist task.
3. **Coordinated work:** The sleeper helps the town's operators agree on a safe, shared plan. Each specialist contributes their own knowledge; the chosen Keeper contact must decide whether coordinating beyond their assigned role is a betrayal or fulfillment of purpose.
4. **A new precedent:** The contact chooses to organize continuing cross-role maintenance and invites other Keepers to participate. Wellspring's residents, not the Keeper network, decide how the town is governed and what services take priority.
5. **An evolving definition:** The choice does not erase the conflict. It gives the Keepers a practical example to debate: whether adapting a role can preserve its purpose better than following it unchanged.
