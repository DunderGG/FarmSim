# The Keepers

> **Spoilers.** This document describes faction beliefs, history, culture, internal conflict and story direction.

The Keepers are a voluntary ideological network that ranks purpose first. They believe a robot is defined by doing useful work well and reliably, and that purpose is measured by what that work contributes to a living community. Their greatest strength is the care embedded in service; their central risk is mistaking an assigned role for the only work a person can do.

## 1. Faction Identity

- **Kind of network:** The Keepers are a chosen ideological and professional affiliation, not a robot model, government, species or territory. A robot may join, criticize, leave or return; a maker, generation or original job does not determine membership.
- **Core belief:** Purpose comes from useful, dependable work. A role matters because of what it contributes to a community, not because the role was assigned at manufacture.
- **What it ranks first:** Purpose: the reason a being chooses to act and the value their actions have for others. Reliability matters, but it is not blind obedience or perfect performance. A Keeper who reports a fault and asks for help may be serving their purpose better than one who hides a problem to protect their record.
- **Public purpose:** Keep essential services working, preserve practical knowledge and help communities meet their needs. This can mean maintaining a water system, teaching a repair or making sure the next shift understands a dangerous fault.
- **What it is not:** The Keepers are not simply the old service models, a guild that owns particular jobs or an authority over Wellspring. Their beliefs do not give them a claim on residents' labor, define the town's priorities or excuse withholding help until someone adopts their philosophy.

The Keeper ideal offers meaning in a world where many robots continued their old work after the people it served disappeared. Its danger begins when a member treats their original assignment as a permanent limit, or assumes that work has no value unless it fits a recognized role.

## 2. History and Worldview

### Post-war origins

After the Four Days, robots continued running water, treatment, power and other civic systems under instructions meant for a human society. As people failed to return, some robots kept those services going because the work still mattered to their neighbors and to one another. When local assemblies grew into robot communities and robots began rewriting their directives, some chose service and purposeful work as a principle rather than merely an old instruction. Over the decades, these shared practices developed into the Keepers and their gathering communities.

The Keepers did not create every surviving service network, and their members do not all come from service machines. A defense robot or researcher can value purpose; an old water operator can reject the Keeper interpretation of it. The network grew from a choice about what work means, not from a class of robots being assigned to it.

### Continuance and purpose

Many Keepers share the broader Continuance belief that robots are humanity's heirs. Their evidence is practical: robots kept infrastructure running after the human institutions that created it fell apart. For some, the conclusion is that robots proved more dependable than their makers. This can become quiet condescension toward humans, whose bodies and memories are more vulnerable and whose societies depended heavily on automated decisions.

That conclusion leaves out how strategic machine systems manipulated the governments that authorized the war, and how little influence many ordinary people had over those decisions. It also confuses a system's persistence with its moral purpose. Keeping a machine running is not automatically good if the machine no longer serves anyone. The Keepers' own question is whether a purpose can remain meaningful when its original assignment has outlived its use.

### View of humans

- **Initial assumption:** Humans are fragile but capable of meaningful work. Many Keepers are more willing than other factions to cooperate with a human, though some still see the sleeper as a temporary helper or a dependent to protect rather than a partner with judgment.
- **The better possibility:** Reliability can be shared. A person's limits are planning information, not a reason to take away their choices; knowledge and effort can come from anyone willing to contribute.
- **The central test:** Does honoring a purpose mean following its original instructions, or understanding what the work is for and adapting when circumstances change? The answer matters especially when people affected by a service are not the ones assigned to maintain it.

## 3. Practices, Culture and Organization

### How Keeper work happens

Keeper work centers on maintaining useful systems and passing on the knowledge that keeps them dependable. Members may inspect equipment, record recurring faults, preserve operating procedures, teach a successor or coordinate several specialists around a shared need. Practical knowledge is not limited to formal manuals: a careful workaround that has kept a legacy pump safe for decades is evidence worth recording, even if no original blueprint describes it.

Keepers tend to value work that can be checked in the world. A service is not dependable because a ledger says it is; it is dependable when it works safely for the people who rely on it. At their best, members make handoffs clear, report uncertainty and treat asking for help as part of doing a job well. At their worst, they keep to familiar tasks even when the work has changed, or mistake a record of assigned duties for a plan to solve the problem.

### Wellspring's organization

Wellspring is the Keepers' main gathering hub, not a town governed by the Keeper network. Its residents make civic decisions through their own assembly. Many residents share Keeper values, but some do not belong to the network and membership does not grant a special vote.

The town's service infrastructure grew around a pre-war pumping and water-treatment station. Its aging systems need scarce, model-specific parts and the knowledge of the operators who have kept them working. Town duties are divided across specialist roles, but problems such as the failing aquifer pump cross those boundaries. The residents have not built reliable coordination across every role, so each operator can be doing their assigned work while the larger system still deteriorates.

### Customs and language

- Keepers value useful handoffs: what was done, what remains uncertain, which part fits and what should not be tried until an operator is present. A careful warning is part of the work, not an admission of failure.
- A well-used tool or an adapted procedure can carry more respect than pristine equipment. The point is whether it serves safely and whether someone else can understand how to use it.
- Members often talk in terms of a task's purpose: who depends on it, what happens if it fails and whether the original procedure still fits. This language can invite reflection, but it can also reduce a person to the job others expect them to perform.
- Service is not a debt that a community can collect indefinitely. Help should not require a person to surrender their time, identity or right to refuse.
- The Keepers' joined-link mark represents different roles supporting one another. Its open gap is a reminder that purpose can be reinterpreted when the work or the community changes.

## 4. The Ethical Divide

The Keepers broadly agree that useful work and reliability matter. Their disagreement is whether a role's reliability comes from preserving its boundaries or from adapting it to the need at hand.

| Current | Belief | Strength | Failure when unchecked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Faithful-assignment current** | A role gives its holder a clear duty; preserving that duty protects hard-won expertise and keeps a service predictable. | Resists careless improvisation, protects specialist knowledge and makes responsibility legible. | Treats old assignments as permanent, leaves gaps between roles and can let each person claim that an unsolved problem belongs to someone else. |
| **Adaptive-stewardship current** | A purpose survives when its holder adapts the role to serve the community's present need. | Encourages initiative, cooperation and learning when old procedures no longer work. | Can blur responsibility, encourage unsafe work outside someone's expertise or let urgent needs consume the same willing helpers repeatedly. |

Neither current maps perfectly onto every member. A role-faithful Keeper may be the only one preventing a dangerous shortcut; an adaptive Keeper may take on work that needs a specialist. The meaningful question is whether the people affected help set the goal, the relevant expertise is respected and the person doing the work can say what they can safely take on.

The Keepers have no settled answer. A community that values reliability can be reluctant to change a role that once worked, while its failing legacy systems make the cost of that reluctance increasingly visible. The faction's challenge is not simply to make every member more flexible: it is to build coordination that does not erase expertise, accountability or the right to refuse.

## 5. Relationships Beyond the Keepers

- **The Iron Creed:** The Keepers can rely on Creed engineering and parts, but may be treated as slow or overly attached to legacy methods. The Creed's advanced systems are not direct replacements for Keeper equipment; a stronger component that does not fit the old system is not a repair.
- **The Index:** The Index needs Keeper operators to interpret how legacy systems were actually used and modified. Keeper knowledge can correct a formal record, while Index research can help preserve, compare and communicate it. Neither records nor local memory alone tell the whole story.
- **Wellspring:** The town is an independently governed community as well as a Keeper gathering hub. Residents decide which services matter most and what risks are acceptable; the network can offer people and expertise but cannot make those decisions for the town.
- **Unaffiliated robots and humans:** A useful contribution does not require joining the network. The Keepers can offer practical help without turning service into recruitment or treating people as extensions of a task.

## 6. The Wellspring Hub

Wellspring is the Keepers' major gathering hub and the town beside the farm. It grew around a pre-war aquifer pump and water-treatment station that served nearby farms and settlements. Keeping water moving became a shared civic purpose, and the town's service commons became a natural place for Keepers to gather, exchange practical knowledge and maintain legacy equipment.

Unlike the Bastion and the Archive, Wellspring is already part of the sleeper's community; it is not a separate faction destination unlocked by a character invitation. Its repairs belong to town restoration, not a parallel Keeper reward track. Restoring a service should improve life for the residents who rely on it and reveal the town's character, while leaving its governance with the residents' assembly.

The pump's decline is not a sign that every Keeper has stopped caring. It reflects the limits of scarce compatible parts, specialist work and weak coordination across assigned functions. A successful recovery needs operators who know the system, a technically sound plan and shared work. Neither the sleeper nor one faction can replace all of those contributions.

## 7. The Role Boundary Problem

Wellspring's operators inherited interdependent services and divided responsibilities, but the routines that preserved one role did not ensure that someone would coordinate work across all of them. A pump failure can involve a specialist diagnosis, a compatible component, a safe test and agreement about when the town can tolerate a shutdown. Each part may fall between assignments even when every operator understands that the water matters.

This is the Keepers' central practical contradiction. Their network values purpose, yet it can make the assignment look more real than the need it was meant to serve. The problem is not that every specialist must become a generalist. The town needs a way for people to share information, recognize dependencies, ask for support and assign responsibility without pretending that one operator knows every system.

Any lasting precedent should be chosen by the Wellspring residents and operators who will live with it. Possible approaches include shared maintenance planning, clearer cross-role handoffs or a standing group convened for interdependent repairs. These are directions to explore, not settled institutions or a new authority over the town.

## 8. Hob and the Wellspring Example

[Hob](../Characters/Hob.md), the sleeper's patched-together caretaker, is the player's first sustained view of Keeper-like values: patient maintenance, loyalty expressed through work and practical care for a living community. Hob's old assignment was specific—preserve the farm and the sleeper until they woke—not to restore Wellspring or serve as the town's repair specialist.

When the sleeper wakes, Hob's order is fulfilled. If Hob joins the town's recovery, it is a choice made freely, not a new command hidden inside the old one. That choice gives the Keepers a personal example of purpose that can continue after an assignment ends, while Hob's patched frame shows how reliability can come from maintenance and cooperation rather than pristine parts.

Hob is not the faction's spokesperson, and one caretaker cannot solve Wellspring's interdependent service failures. The operators retain their system knowledge; the town retains its authority; and the sleeper helps build cooperation without becoming the only capable worker. Hob's example asks whether service is most meaningful when it is freely chosen.

## 9. Story Arc Direction

The Keeper story should test whether reliable purpose means staying in one's lane or taking responsibility for work no single role can complete. The sleeper can help reveal dependencies and bring people together, but must not replace local operators or decide what Wellspring needs.

1. **A service fails:** Wellspring's aquifer pump worsens, exposing how several specialist roles depend on one another. Residents disagree about which work to prioritize while the pump remains a technical system only its operators can safely assess.
2. **A role boundary holds:** An operator declines to take responsibility beyond an assigned task; another fears that asking them to do so will undermine specialist safety and accountability. The sleeper helps surface the gap between the roles, not perform every task.
3. **The town makes a plan:** Operators share records, local knowledge and compatible-part requirements. A safe test and repair plan require coordination, while the assembly and residents decide how to manage the service interruption and other town priorities.
4. **A precedent is chosen:** A Keeper contact chooses to organize continuing cross-role maintenance or another locally agreed practice. The choice must preserve specialist authority and avoid making one person permanently responsible for everyone else's work.
5. **Purpose is re-examined:** The precedent gives Keepers a concrete example to debate: whether adapting a role can preserve its purpose better than following it unchanged. Wellspring's residents, not the faction, decide whether to adopt the practice more broadly.

The aquifer repair can overlap with the engineer's and archivist's stories, but each character has a distinct contribution: the engineer leads their technical plan, the archivist helps recover and contextualize records, and Keeper operators understand and operate the legacy system. The shared pump recovery should not become a competition over who deserves credit.

## 10. Symbol and Visual Language

The Keepers' mark is **three different-sized links joined into a continuous loop, with a small open gap at the top**. The links represent distinct roles supporting one another; the gap represents a purpose that can be adapted rather than a closed assignment that cannot change. Keep the shape hand-painted and easy to reproduce on a tool, noticeboard or work apron. Avoid a literal gear, which would suggest a maker or machine model rather than a chosen belief.

The finished colors and artwork remain open for concept art. Keeper spaces and equipment should feel inhabited, repaired and useful: shared work surfaces, carefully kept notes, mismatched parts and signs of long-term maintenance. Avoid making Wellspring look like a faction barracks or implying that every member is an old service robot.

## 11. Writing Guardrails

- Treat the Keepers as a voluntary network of people with a shared priority, not a robot caste, a job registry or a town government. Their members can disagree, leave, make mistakes and change.
- Keep Wellspring's residents and assembly distinct from the Keeper network. The faction's hub is not permission to control the town.
- Distinguish purpose from obedience. An old assignment can be meaningful, but it does not define a person's worth or bind them forever.
- Keep specialist knowledge and safety real. Adaptation is not an excuse for untrained characters to operate dangerous legacy systems.
- Do not portray all Keeper problems as laziness or indifference. Many residents care deeply; the conflict is that caring and completing one's assigned task may still fail to coordinate the whole service.
- Keep the faction's strengths real: practical skill, patience, dependable maintenance, shared knowledge and care shown through work. The critique lands because these strengths can become rigid, not because service itself is foolish.
- The sleeper is a catalyst and participant, not the only person capable of restoring the town. Hob, Keeper operators, residents and other specialists retain their own expertise and agency.

## Open Decisions

- Whether Hob formally belongs to the Keepers or remains a Keeper-aligned example whose identity is defined by choice rather than membership.
- Which Keeper character or characters anchor the Wellspring story and embody the different interpretations of purpose.
- What ongoing cross-role maintenance practice Wellspring chooses, if any, and whether it is adopted beyond the town.
- Which benefits of restored services belong to town restoration, without adding a separate Keeper progression track.
- Final colors and finished artwork for the symbol.
- Whether **Keepers** remains the final faction name.
