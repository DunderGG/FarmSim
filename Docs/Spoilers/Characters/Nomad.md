# The nomad

> **Spoilers.** This document describes a character's story and role in the world.

The nomad is a rare, recurring human visitor who trades unusual wasteland salvage and news. They keep a small, fragile circuit of consent-based contacts between scattered survivors, and know the wasteland's routes better than most robots. Their story asks whether independence has to mean carrying every responsibility alone.

## Character

- **Who:** Name, gender and pronouns undecided. A human born after the war who has never known the pre-war world. They are a trader, route-finder and careful carrier of news, not an explorer looking to claim new territory.
- **Appearance:** A practical travel rig built up over many journeys: dust goggles, a scarf that can cover the nose and mouth, layered weatherproof clothes, sturdy boots, a rolled bedroll and a pack of labelled pouches. Their hand-drawn route book is as important as any piece of salvage, though it records hazards, landmarks and agreed signals rather than a complete map to every person they know. The person beneath the gear should look alert, tired and approachable when they choose to be; their kit is maintained because failure on a route has consequences for more than one person.
- **View of humans and robots:** The nomad does not treat the sleeper as a representative of humanity or as an answer to what humans used to be. They are curious about the sleeper's memories, but wary of being treated as an exhibit or as proof that humans can be trusted. They are equally unwilling to sort robots into simple allies or enemies: a robot can honour a bargain, and a human can endanger a route. They judge both by what they do with the information and trust they are given.
- **Backstory:** The nomad grew up on one leg of a small human-run circuit between concealed refuges and cautious trading stops. The circuit never connected every survivor; it survived precisely because no one person carried a complete list of people and locations. Older runners taught the nomad to read storm fronts, spot a road that had started drawing curfew patrols, maintain a cache and tell the difference between useful news and dangerous gossip. When fewer people were able or willing to travel, the nomad took on longer runs. What began as a way to keep their own stop supplied became a livelihood carrying filters, medicine, tools, letters and carefully verified warnings.
- **Want:** To keep the route viable and their freedom to travel intact. Trading lets them acquire supplies without settling in one place or relying on a single community. More personally, they want to be useful to the people who trusted them without becoming the sole person responsible for keeping all of them safe.
- **Fear and contradiction:** The nomad fears a failed route, a hostile group or a copied route note exposing someone who expected privacy. They respond by keeping contacts compartmentalized and by refusing help that comes with demands for names or locations. That caution protects people, but it also leaves them carrying problems alone and makes the network too dependent on their memory and judgment. They pride themselves on needing no one while depending on fair traders, maintained shelters and people who keep their word.
- **Personality and voice:** Observant, dryly funny and economical with personal information. The nomad notices water levels, missing trail markers and what has been repaired since their last visit before they comment on the weather. They bargain firmly but do not exploit a desperate need, and they are quicker to offer a practical warning than a comforting speech. When a question crosses a boundary, they say so plainly rather than inventing a story. Their warmth is most visible in small acts: returning a repaired tool, remembering a preference or leaving a useful fact with no expectation of payment.
- **Role in the game:** A rare, recurring visitor and the player's main contact for unusual wasteland salvage. They offer items the player is unlikely to find through routine farming or Wellspring's shops, in exchange for currency, useful goods or salvage. Their stock is limited and opportunistic; they are not the sole source of essential progression items. Their news can point to optional caches, changed hazards and story opportunities, but never functions as a complete catalogue of human settlements.
- **Player's role:** The player can make the farm a useful, safe stop, bring back salvage the nomad values and share what they learn about newly opened routes. Fair dealing means respecting a declined question or a sealed delivery as well as paying a good price. The nomad decides which routes to travel, what information to pass on and whether the farm is safe enough to add to their circuit; the player earns trust through reliable boundaries rather than by rescuing or recruiting them.
- **Personal arc and key events:**
  1. **The first measure:** The nomad reaches the farm after a difficult crossing and treats it as a one-time trade stop, not a place to interrogate or recruit. They test the sleeper's judgment with an ordinary but specific agreement: a fair exchange, a sealed item left sealed, or a warning passed on without demanding its source. The player learns that a useful relationship starts with respecting a limit.
  2. **The silent marker:** A familiar route marker does not receive its expected reply. The nomad cannot tell whether a contact has moved, a storm destroyed the cache or someone has learned how the circuit works. The sleeper can help investigate the surrounding wasteland, compare weather records or recover a lost supply case, but the nomad decides how much of the situation it is safe to explain.
  3. **The copied page:** Evidence shows that part of an old route note has reached someone who wants the circuit's information without accepting its responsibilities. The immediate question is not whether the player can defeat a villain, but whether the nomad should close a useful route, warn every contact and risk causing panic, or trust a few people with a safer replacement. The sleeper can offer a secure farm stop and share practical evidence; the nomad chooses the response and accepts its cost.
  4. **The new stop:** The nomad asks whether the farm can become a limited, clearly agreed place for rest, trade and warnings. Hob, Wellspring and the sleeper each have a reason to be cautious, and the nomad refuses to promise access to hidden communities in return. After the player helps establish the conditions, the nomad chooses to add the farm to the circuit under rules that protect both the farm and the people who use it.
  5. **The open route:** The repaired circuit no longer rests on the nomad alone: trusted stops can confirm hazards, hold supplies and pass only the information their owners have agreed to share. The nomad remains a traveller by choice, not because no one expects them home. They leave on the next run with a copied, deliberately incomplete route book and the knowledge that asking for support is not the same as surrendering their freedom.
- **Arc outcome and unlocks:** Friendship reveals selected route notes, the care behind the nomad's redactions and the people whose consent makes each exchange possible. It expands the variety of trade and makes their news more dependable, while preserving the privacy of contacts who have not agreed to meet the sleeper. The farm becomes one voluntarily maintained stop in a mutual-support network, not a headquarters or a shortcut to a hidden human society.
- **Romance:** Undecided. The character is complete as a trader and friend. If they become a love interest, the romance should be opt-in and about choosing a relationship that leaves room for travel, consent and separate responsibilities; it should not require them to abandon the route or be "settled" by the sleeper.
