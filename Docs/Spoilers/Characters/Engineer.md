# The engineer

> **Spoilers.** This document describes a character's story and personal arc.

The engineer is an Iron Creed member visiting Wellspring through a parts-and-expertise exchange with the Bastion. His work on the failing aquifer pump brings him, the Keeper operators and the sleeper into a practical collaboration; engineering is his broader skill, not just his assignment here.

## Character

- **Who:** Name and model undecided. An engineer and voluntary Iron Creed member who came to Wellspring through a parts-and-expertise exchange with the Bastion.
- **Gender and pronouns:** Identifies as a man and uses he/its pronouns. His identity is independent of his frame.
- **Appearance:** A compact, heavy-duty frame built for practical work, with reinforced joints and a few visible field repairs. One forearm carries interchangeable engineering tools and a rugged diagnostic display. His equipment is orderly and maintained, but not pristine; a restrained Bastion mark identifies his affiliation without making him look like a standard-issue soldier. His maker and model remain open, and his Iron Creed membership is ideological rather than determined by his chassis.
- **Faction and view of humans:** Values readiness, discipline and protecting others. Believes that humanity's dependence on automated decisions helped bring about the war, and doubts that the sleeper can contribute safely to delicate infrastructure work. This caution has a real safety basis, but he wrongly treats human vulnerability as evidence that a human cannot take responsibility.
- **Backstory:** He has spent much of his working life on field repairs for the Bastion and chose to join the Iron Creed for its emphasis on maintenance, preparation and service. Early in his career, he misread a fault during a rushed repair. A second failure disabled his frame before anyone was hurt; a fellow engineer pulled him clear, and the team returned together to finish the job. He documented his mistake and changed his inspection routine. He came away believing that disciplined checks and dependable partners make a body ready for danger. He still has trouble extending that lesson to humans: he sees a robot accepting help as responsible interdependence, but a human needing help as proof of human unreliability.
- **Temperament and voice:** Methodical, dry-humored and sparing with praise. He checks a tool twice before using it and prefers a concrete observation to a confident guess. Under pressure he becomes formal and gives short instructions; when relaxed, he makes understated jokes about things that are "within tolerance." He is more comfortable showing care through preparation and follow-up work than saying that he is worried.
- **Relationships:** He respects Keeper operators' practical knowledge but initially mistakes their role boundaries and slow coordination for a lack of initiative. With the sleeper, he starts by setting firm safety limits and gradually learns to distinguish a useful warning from a dismissive assumption. At the Bastion, he wants his peers to trust his judgment; he fears that reporting uncertainty will make the exchange look like a failure.
- **Want:** To return to the Bastion with a precise assessment and a repair plan that can be carried out without risking the town's water supply. He also wants to be trusted as a capable engineer, not treated as a symbol of Iron Creed policy.
- **Fear and contradiction:** Fears that a rushed or politically motivated repair could leave Wellspring worse off. He insists that accepting help from the sleeper would be irresponsible, while his own formative experience taught him that reliable partners are part of readiness. He wants the pump fixed but initially treats collaboration with a human as a concession rather than a strength.
- **Role in the game:** A technical partner in Wellspring's pump recovery. The sleeper helps gather evidence, locate compatible components and coordinate with Keeper operators; the engineer remains responsible for his own judgments and technical work. The pump is repaired through shared effort, not by the sleeper acting as a lone expert.
- **Personal arc and key events:**
  1. **The assessment:** The engineer inspects the failing pump and tells the sleeper to stay clear; an incorrect intervention could interrupt Wellspring's water supply. The sleeper can accept the boundary or challenge the engineer to explain what evidence would change his mind. The engineer agrees to review useful observations, but remains responsible for the technical work.
  2. **The missing record:** The engineer's initial diagnosis is limited by incomplete maintenance records and incompatible legacy parts. The sleeper helps recover a relevant record or component from the surrounding area. It reveals a risk the engineer had not accounted for, not that humans are inherently better engineers. The engineer revises the plan and acknowledges that dismissing the sleeper also meant dismissing potentially useful evidence.
  3. **The controlled test:** A Keeper operator identifies a safe test that requires coordination between roles. The engineer must choose between delaying for the operator and proceeding with an incomplete test. The sleeper can help bring the parties together and weigh the consequences, but cannot make the technical decision for the engineer. The engineer chooses to wait, and the test exposes a second failure before it can damage the pump.
  4. **The report:** The Bastion expects a concise assessment and may interpret a request for Keeper support as weakness or a failed assignment. The engineer considers understating the uncertainty to protect his standing. The sleeper can encourage candour or focus on the political risk; either way, the engineer decides to report the limitations honestly and propose a joint repair team. The Bastion's response includes an invitation for the sleeper to visit; the engineer shares the route and offers to guide them there, making the hub's location known and opening it as a destination.
  5. **The repair and choice:** Keeper operators, the engineer and the sleeper carry out the agreed repair using compatible parts gathered through the story. The engineer leads the technical plan, while the others contribute their distinct knowledge and work. Afterward, the engineer chooses to recommend continued parts-and-expertise exchanges between Wellspring and the Bastion, treating cooperation as readiness that makes both communities more resilient.
- **Arc outcome:** The pump recovery is a shared achievement, not a test of whether the sleeper can replace trained specialists. The engineer changes his view from excluding a human by default to judging the sleeper's contributions on their merits. The town's trust in the engineer grows because he takes responsibility for an honest, cooperative plan. He remains an Iron Creed member and does not have to abandon its emphasis on strength to make that change.
- **Friendship and romance:** Friendship reveals the engineer's private notes about the risks he weighed, the repair that first taught him to trust a partner, and the pressure he feels to appear certain. The engineer is a love interest; romance is optional and grows from mutual respect, trust and chosen vulnerability, not through the sleeper proving their worth by being technically useful.
